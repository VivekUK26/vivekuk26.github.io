Leadership Philosophy

How I think about delivery leadership.

—01

Delivery is a people problem first.

Tools and frameworks don't deliver programmes. People do. When a delivery is struggling, nine times out of ten the root cause is unclear ownership or a team that doesn't feel safe flagging problems early. That's always where I start before touching anything else.

—02

Technical credibility isn’t optional at senior level.

Ten years writing code and designing systems means I don't just sit in technical reviews and nod along. I can push back on an estimate, question an architecture call, and tell when scope is being undercooked. Engineers pick up on that quickly, and it changes how the whole conversation goes.

—03

Compliance is a competitive advantage, not a tax.

Treating compliance as a late-stage concern is expensive. I've run programmes where it was built in from day one, and ones where it was scrambled in at the end. The first kind has fewer nasty surprises, cleaner audits, and stakeholders who actually sleep at night. The difference is usually just planning and discipline.

—04

AI handles the noise so you can focus on the signal.

Automated status rollups, early risk flagging, smarter reporting. These are real, useful things GenAI can do in delivery right now. The decisions that actually need judgement still need a person. Getting that balance right, knowing what to hand off and what to hold, is what I'm working on.
